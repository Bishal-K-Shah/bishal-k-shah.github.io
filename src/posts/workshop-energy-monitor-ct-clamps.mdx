## Introduction: The High-Power Workshop

A workshop isn't like a living room. While a TV or a laptop pulls a few dozen watts, a MIG welder, a 60-gallon air compressor, or a heavy-duty wood lathe can pull thousands of watts in an instant. This high power consumption presents three challenges:

1. **Cost:** Running a 5HP compressor all day isn't cheap.
2. **Safety:** Overloading a circuit can lead to tripped breakers or, worse, electrical fires.
3. **Logistics:** If you know exactly when your compressor is cycling too frequently, you might catch a major air leak before it burns out the motor.

Standard "smart plugs" are rated for 15A at 120V—totally useless for a 240V welder. To monitor these beasts, we need **Current Transformer (CT) Clamps**. They are non-invasive, safe, and can measure hundreds of amps without you ever touching a bare wire.

In this guide, we’ll build a multi-channel energy monitor using an ESP32 and ESPHome to track your shop's heaviest hitters.

---

## 1. Electrical Safety: Read This First!

Working around electrical panels and 240V lines is dangerous. While CT clamps are "non-invasive" (they clip *around* the wire, not into it), you still need to open your electrical panel or a junction box to install them.

> **CRITICAL WARNING:** 
> - **Never** open your electrical panel if you are not comfortable and knowledgeable about high-voltage safety.
> - **Always** turn off the main breaker before removing the panel cover.
> - Even with the main breaker off, the **lugs coming into the top of the panel are still live**. 
> - If you have any doubt, hire a licensed electrician to clip the clamps on for you. It’s a 5-minute job for them.

## 3. How CT Clamps Work (The 30-Second Theory)

A CT clamp is basically a transformer. When AC current flows through a wire, it creates a magnetic field. The clamp "catches" this field and induces a much smaller current in its own coil. 

**Important:** A CT clamp must only go around **one** of the wires (the hot wire). if you clip it around a whole power cord (hot + neutral), the fields cancel each other out, and you get a reading of zero.

---

## 4. Hardware Wiring: The Bias Circuit

The ESP32’s analog pins can only read voltages between 0V and 3.3V. However, the output of a CT clamp is an AC wave that goes positive and negative. We need a "DC Bias" circuit to shift that wave so it sits right in the middle (at 1.65V).

### The Circuit Diagram (Conceptual)
1. Two 100k resistors create a voltage divider between 3.3V and GND.
2. A 10uF capacitor stabilizes this 1.65V midpoint.
3. One leg of the CT clamp connects to this 1.65V midpoint.
4. The other leg goes to the ESP32 (or ADS1115) analog input.

> **Pro Tip:** Use the **ADS1115 ADC module** instead of the ESP32’s built-in analog pins. The ESP32's ADC is notoriously "noisy" and non-linear, which makes energy monitoring much less accurate.

## 5. ESPHome Configuration: Calibration and Logic

We’ll use the `ct_clamp` component in ESPHome. This component samples the AC wave at high speed and calculates the Root Mean Square (RMS) current.

### The YAML Configuration

```yaml
sensor:
  - platform: ads1115
    multiplexer: 'A0_GND'
    gain: 4.096
    name: "ADS1115 Channel 0"
    update_interval: 10s
    id: ads_ch0

  - platform: ct_clamp
    sensor: ads_ch0
    name: "Welder Current"
    update_interval: 10s
    unit_of_measurement: "A"
    # Calibration: clip it on a known 100W lightbulb (0.83A) 
    # and adjust the 'to' value until it matches.
    filters:
      - calibrate_linear:
          - 0 -> 0
          - 0.05 -> 0.83 
      - multiply: 240 # Convert Amps to Watts for 240V
    id: welder_power

  - platform: template
    name: "Welder Power (Watts)"
    unit_of_measurement: "W"
    lambda: return id(welder_power).state;
```

## 6. Home Assistant Dashboard and Analysis

Once your data is flowing into Home Assistant, you can create some truly useful insights.

### Identifying "The Leaks"
If your air compressor power graph shows a 3000W spike every 2 hours while you aren't in the shop, **you have an air leak**. Over time, those 2-minute cycles add up to significant electricity costs and wear and tear on the pump.

### Automations for Safety
```yaml
alias: "Warning: Welder Left On"
trigger:
  - platform: numeric_state
    entity_id: sensor.welder_power
    above: 10
    for: "01:00:00" # If welder is drawing power for > 1 hour
action:
  - service: notify.mobile_app_phone
    data:
      title: "Workshop Alert"
      message: "The welder has been active for over an hour. Did you forget to turn it off?"
```
