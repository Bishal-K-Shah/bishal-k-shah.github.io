Every watt your homelab draws 24/7 costs roughly $1/year (US) or $2.50/year (EU). Before you can optimize, you need to measure. This guide covers tools and methods to track homelab power consumption—from a simple plug-in meter to Grafana dashboards.

For real N100 power numbers and hardware comparison, see our [N100 power consumption guide](/blog/low-power-homelab-n100-mini-pc).

## Why Track Homelab Power?

- **Cost** – See exactly what each server or stack costs per year
- **Optimization** – Find energy hogs and unnecessary 24/7 services
- **Validation** – Confirm that "low power" claims match reality
- **Trends** – Spot when a device starts drawing more (fans, failing PSU)

## Tools for Measuring Power

| Tool | Cost | Accuracy | Best For |
|------|------|----------|----------|
| **Kill-A-Watt (plug-in)** | $20–35 | Good | Single device, spot checks |
| **Smart plug with energy** | $15–30 | Good | Per-outlet, remote monitoring |
| **Shelly Plug / Emporia Vue** | $20–50 | Good | Local API, no cloud |
| **PDU with metering** | $80–200+ | Excellent | Rack, per-outlet |
| **CT clamps** | $30–80 | Excellent | Whole circuit (see our [workshop CT clamp guide](/blog/workshop-energy-monitor-ct-clamps) for 240V tools) |

For homelab servers on 120V outlets, a Kill-A-Watt or smart plug is usually enough. For a full rack, a metered PDU is better.

## Kill-A-Watt: Simple Spot Checks

Plug the Kill-A-Watt between the wall and your server. It shows:

- **Watts** – Instantaneous draw
- **kWh** – Cumulative energy (reset it weekly or monthly to track)
- **Voltage, frequency** – Useful for diagnosing power issues

Leave it connected for 24–48 hours to get an average. Multiply watts × 24 × 365 / 1000 for annual kWh, then multiply by your electricity rate.

## Smart Plugs with Energy Monitoring

Plugs like TP-Link Kasa, Shelly Plug S, or Emporia Vue measure watts and often kWh. Advantages:

- **Remote** – Check power from your phone or dashboard
- **Historical** – Some store days or weeks of data
- **Automation** – Integrate with Home Assistant to log to InfluxDB or Prometheus

**Shelly Plug S** has a local REST API—no cloud required. Good for homelab privacy.

## Grafana Dashboard (Advanced)

If your smart plug exposes data via MQTT, REST, or a local API:

1. **Collect** – Use Telegraf, Node-RED, or a simple script to poll and write to InfluxDB or Prometheus
2. **Store** – InfluxDB for time-series, or Prometheus if you already use it
3. **Visualize** – Grafana dashboard with watts, kWh, and cost over time

Example: Shelly Plug → MQTT → Node-RED → InfluxDB → Grafana. Plenty of guides exist for each step.

## Calculating Cost

| Formula | Example |
|---------|---------|
| **Annual kWh** | Watts × 24 × 365 / 1000 |
| **Annual cost (US)** | kWh × $0.12 |
| **Annual cost (EU)** | kWh × $0.30 |

Example: 10W × 24 × 365 / 1000 = 87.6 kWh. At $0.12/kWh, that is **$10.51/year**.

Start with a Kill-A-Watt or smart plug on your main homelab outlet. Once you have numbers, you can optimize—consolidate services, replace power hogs, or add a Grafana dashboard for ongoing tracking.
